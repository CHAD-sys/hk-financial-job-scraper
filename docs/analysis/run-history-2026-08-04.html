The night shift is not a night shift

The pipeline is described as daily and named daily_run.sh. Over 21 calendar days it ran 15 times, starting at nine different hours of the day, with gaps as long as 85 hours. This is not a cron job — it is someone launching a script when they remember to.

reliability

A quarter of runs die silently, and nothing notices.

Four runs logged === Daily pipeline started === and never logged the matching finish — two during description fetching, two during scraping. Because the summary email is the last phase, an aborted run sends no email. The failure signal is the absence of a message, which is exactly the signal a human is worst at noticing. The email phase itself started 13 times but confirmed delivery only 8.

variance

Run duration varies nine-fold with no change in workload.

The fastest complete run took 24.6 minutes; the slowest took 230.3. Both scraped the same ~248 companies. The spread is not driven by volume — it is driven by how many detail fetches hit a Cloudflare challenge and retry. Duration is a proxy for bot-defence luck, not for how much work there was.

instrumentation

The log double-counts one run, so the obvious way to count runs gives the wrong answer.

On 25 July the pipeline wrote its start marker twice and its finish marker twice, at identical timestamps — one run, logged by two handlers. Any script that counts === Daily pipeline started === reports 16 runs where there are 15, and reports a phantom aborted run that never existed. This page's first pass made exactly that mistake. If run counts ever feed a dashboard, deduplicate on the timestamp first.

cost of attention

The Secret Market costs 13% of pipeline time for 4.5% of the board — and it is the only inventory nobody else has.

Fetching and promoting LinkedIn recruiter posts takes 116.8 minutes of the 920 minutes tracked. It yields 230 active roles out of 5,123. On raw yield that is a bad trade. But every one of those 230 is flagged truly hidden — not present on any board the pipeline scrapes — and 99.6% carry extraction confidence ≥ 0.7. This is the only line item that buys differentiation rather than volume, and it is the one the time budget should protect.

What the data says about itself

Three defects in the enrichment layer. The first one matters most, because it affects the single number a job seeker is most likely to act on.

the headline defect

"High confidence" does not mean the salary is well-evidenced. It means the role is junior.

Only 73 of 12,819 enrichments carry a high confidence label — and none of them has an actual salary stated on the underlying posting. Meanwhile 1,204 medium rows do have a stated salary and were never upgraded. The label tracks how narrow the model believes the salary band to be, and junior bands are narrow. A user reading "high confidence" reasonably infers "we checked this" — the opposite is true. 

conf     n      stated salary on posting   avg max
high     73     0                          31,697
medium   9,694  1,204                      73,584
low      1,129  54                         47,284
none     1,923  54                              —

high-confidence rows by seniority:
  junior 59  ·  mid 12  ·  senior 2

Cheapest fix: stop surfacing this field, or rename it to what it measures — band width — and derive a real evidence flag from jobs.salary_min IS NOT NULL, which is already stored and is true for 1,431 rows.

non-comparable

A third of the corpus was enriched by a different model under a different prompt.

8,602 rows carry the v10 merged prompt on deepseek-v4-flash. 4,217 rows — 32.9% — carry a NULL prompt version on legacy deepseek-chat. Both populate the same columns, and every aggregate on this page silently mixes them. Any salary or seniority comparison across the board is comparing two different instruments until the legacy third is re-enriched.

entity resolution

Bank of China is three companies, DBS is two, and the trend metrics believe it.

315 distinct company strings collapse to 303 once you strip Limited, Ltd, punctuation and brackets. Ten collision groups cover roughly 3,800 job rows. This is not cosmetic: company_metrics keys on the raw name, so a single employer's postings are split across rows and each fragment gets its own 7-day growth rate. coverage

One row in four has no description at all, and that is mostly by design.

4,060 of 15,329 rows carry an empty description_clean. Indeed accounts for 1,952 of them (73.3% of its own rows) because the adapter is listing-only by design — no per-job detail fetch. JobsDB accounts for another 1,847. These rows can be listed but cannot be meaningfully enriched: skills, seniority and salary are all inferred from a title alone.

Which sources actually earn their place

Every source costs run time, error volume and legal exposure. Ranked by what survives to the board — and by how long it survives — the ordering is not the one the configuration implies.

best source

eFinancialCareers is the highest-quality source and it is under-weighted.

It is the only source that is simultaneously low-churn (35.4% closed vs 78.7% for JobsDB), richly described (5 empty descriptions out of 1,291, averaging 3,483 characters) and legally uncomplicated relative to the JobsDB/Indeed posture. It runs against 23 configured employers — fewer than a third of the JobsDB count.

poor trade

The long tail is 27% of the estate and 3% of the board.

68 boutiques — over a quarter of all enabled companies — are scraped by LLM extraction from their own careers pages. Across the window, 819 of 2,473 page extractions returned literally zero jobs: a third of the LLM calls on this path produce nothing. The adapter has contributed 407 rows in its lifetime, of which 151 are open.

silent failure

About 45 sources are down on any given day, and the count is stable enough to be structural.

The daily digest reports 42, 48, 45 and 45 sources down on its four most recent sends — roughly 18% of the 248 enabled companies, every day. 124 distinct company slugs logged "returned 0 jobs" at least once. pimco-hk did it 108 times. The storage layer correctly refuses to deactivate their jobs on a zero-return, so the failures cause no data loss — they just never get fixed, because a stable number looks like a healthy number.

working well

Cross-source deduplication is quietly doing a fifth of the work.

4,889 rows (31.9%) were detected as cross-posted across sources, and 3,093 of them (20.2% of the corpus) are held as non-primary duplicates. Without it, one in five listings on the board would be a repeat of another. This is the least visible part of the pipeline and the most obviously load-bearing.

cross-posted, primary      1,796   11.7%

classifier

The model has effectively four seniority labels, not seven.

mid, senior, junior and lead account for 12,810 of 12,819 rows. director was assigned three times, associate once. Either Hong Kong finance posts almost no director-level roles publicly — plausible, since those move through search firms — or the prompt's label set is wider than the model will actually use. Worth a spot-check either way.

If you fix four things

Ordered by damage prevented per hour spent.

1  ·  Make a failed run louder than a successful one.

The summary email is the last phase, so failure is signalled by silence. Move a heartbeat to the start of the run and alert on a missing finish marker instead. This is the difference between "31% of runs failed" and "31% of runs failed and I found out three weeks later."

2  ·  Retire or rename salary_estimated_confidence.

It is anti-correlated with actual salary evidence and it is displayed to job seekers. The honest replacement already exists in the schema: whether jobs.salary_min is populated.

3  ·  Normalise company names before company_metrics reads them.

Ten collision groups, ~3,800 rows, and every trend number on the board is computed on the fragments. A slug normaliser at write time fixes the metrics, the "Listed on" tags and the employer pages in one move.

4  ·  Decide what the long tail is for.

27% of the configured estate returns 3% of the board with a 33% zero-extraction rate. That is defensible if the goal is coverage of boutiques nobody else lists — it is not defensible as a volume play. Either narrow it to the boutiques that actually post, or accept the cost explicitly and stop measuring it on yield.
