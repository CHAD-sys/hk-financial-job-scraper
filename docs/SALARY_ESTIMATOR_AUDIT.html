The verdict

Read this first

The estimator is well-calibrated in aggregate and unreliable per listing. Weighted to the population, its midpoint sits 3.2% below Sonnet's — for a market-level statistic, that is a good number, and the median per-job deviation is exactly 0.0%. If you are publishing "the median HK compliance role pays X", the figure holds.

But a job seeker does not read the aggregate; they read one card. At that level the two estimators disagree by more than 25% on a third of listings, and their bands fail to overlap at all on 32%. The dispersion — not the bias — is the problem.

Underneath that dispersion sits one defect class that is not noise, is currently visible on the public board, and is already prohibited by the estimator's own prompt: internships priced as full-time front-office roles.

Findings

Ordered by severity

Critical

Internships are priced as full-time analysts, against an explicit rule

STEP 1 of the production prompt — the very first instruction, before any tier table — says an internship is capped at HK$3,000–12,000/month, never above HK$15,000, and spells out the whole-word matching so that "Internal Audit" is not caught by mistake. The rule is well written. It is being ignored.

Of 153 internship, graduate-programme and placement listings live on the board, 58 (38%) exceed the HK$15,000 cap. The median breach is HK$37,500. Eight sit at HK$41,500–83,500 — Citi, Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank, Standard Chartered, Barclays summer programmes for 2027.

The failure is specific and legible: the model reads the desk correctly (investment banking, global markets), maps it to the right tier table, then applies the full-time Analyst band because that is the lowest row the tier table contains. Seniority is separately (and correctly) stored as junior on every one of these rows — so the signal needed to catch this is already in the record.

High

The salary audit agent looks straight at these and approves them

Of the 47 intern rows priced at HK$30,000 or more, 21 were never selected for audit — they fall under the HK$120,000 bar and match none of the ambiguous-title patterns in _select_outliers. Of the 26 that were audited, 14 came back ok (11 were lowered, 1 flagged upward).

Electronic Trading maps to global markets trading and the estimate sits within the Analyst band, the lowest appropriate grade for a summer internship. salary_audit_log — verdict "ok" on HK$35,500–57,000

The auditor recognises it is a summer internship and approves anyway, because its prompt asks only whether the tier and grade are right. Employment class is not in its remit, and the internship cap does not appear in its prompt at all. Both layers of defence share the same blind spot.

Medium

Lead roles still run hot — the exact thing the audit was built for

The lead stratum is the only one with a material directional bias: production sits +15.1% above Sonnet, and its bands overlap Sonnet's on just 57% of listings, the worst of any group. The offenders are the titles salary_audit.py was written to catch — "Team Head, Relationship Management" at +112%, "Team Leader, FCC Investigations" at +187%, "Section Head, Treasury Settlement" at +94%.

The audit is running (2,277 verdicts, 482 corrections applied) and its downward-only design is sound. It is simply not reaching these. Worth noting the prompt's own preamble states the estimator "has historically run about 30% too high" — at lead level that correction has not fully landed.

Medium

Stated confidence is genuinely calibrated — use it in the UI

This one is good news, and it is unusual enough to be worth acting on. The estimator's own confidence field predicts its accuracy cleanly:

Low

Two small data defects worth a line of code each

Years of experience parsed as pay. Six of the 55 rows carrying a disclosed salary hold impossible values — 2–3, 1–2, 8–10 (plainly years), plus 380,000–450,000 and 833,333 (annual figures left unconverted). All six come from linkedin_posts.

A null minimum with a non-null maximum. One sampled row — "Investment Data & Operations Associate" — carries salary_estimated_min = NULL alongside a high confidence and a max of 37,000. Whatever renders a range on the job card has to handle that.

What I would fix, in order

Ranked by damage per unit of effort

01
Clamp internships deterministically, in code, not in the prompt.

The rule is already written and the model ignores it on 38% of cases. hk_jobs/salary_clamp.py already exists as the place where a hard ceiling belongs — a whole-word title match plus a HK$15,000 cap fixes 58 live listings today and every future one, with no model call and no new spend.

02
Add employment class to the audit's selection and prompt.

_select_outliers should flag any internship priced above the cap regardless of the HK$120,000 bar, and the audit prompt needs the internship rule pasted into it — otherwise it will keep returning ok on roles it correctly identifies as internships.

03
Soften or suppress the figure on low-confidence rows.

668 live rows where the estimate agrees with an independent model less than half the time. Confidence is calibrated well enough to drive this directly.

04
Sanity-bound the disclosed-salary parser.

Reject a parsed monthly figure below HK$5,000 or above HK$250,000 at write time, and convert obvious annual figures. Six bad rows today, and this field is ground truth for every future calibration — it should not be the noisy one.

What shipped

Resolved 2026-08-18 — this section added after the fix went live

The internship ceiling now lives in hk_jobs/salary_clamp.py, where a prompt cannot decline to apply it. It is enforced after every ladder ceiling, so it wins over the full-time band that caused the problem, and it suppresses the floor-raise step that would otherwise hand that band straight back. Matching is whole-word, so "Internal Audit" and "International" cannot trigger it.

138 published listings were corrected — 66 on the live board, then 72 more sitting off it. That second number is the part worth remembering: a soft-deleted Role keeps its estimate and returns with it when a later scrape re-activates it, so a board-only backfill would have leaked wrong figures back onto the board for months. A repeat run now reports zero rows to change.

Two rows were deliberately not corrected. Weath Management Manager / Wealth Management Intern is one listing advertising two jobs; the title matches the internship rule but the cap would be wrong for the Manager half. The repair now requires the stored seniority to agree with the title before it rewrites a published salary, and reports the disagreements instead of writing them.

Total model cost of the correction: zero. The clamp is deterministic and the repair recomputes from stored rows, so no DeepSeek call was made — confirmed by ai_usage.calls == 0 in every run record. Folding the change into salary.version() instead would have re-enriched ~13,000 rows.

The other findings below are unchanged and still open: lead roles run hot, and the 668 low-confidence estimates remain a UI decision rather than a data fix.

Method, and what it cannot tell you

So the numbers can be judged

Population. The 4,256 live published listings carrying an estimate — is_active = 1 AND is_primary = 1, the same BOARD rule job_read.py serves. All 4,256 share one model and one prompt version, so nothing here is version drift.

Sample. 150 listings, seeded and reproducible, stratified by seniority with a floor of 30 per stratum so lead is measurable. Because that over-samples small strata, every population figure is design-weighted by N/n; the per-stratum rows are unweighted.

Blinding. Sonnet received title, company, location, employment type and description only — never the existing estimate, and never the stored seniority, which is itself model output. It ran the production prompt verbatim, at the same 2,000-character description cap.

The limitation that matters. Sonnet is a second opinion, not truth. Where the two disagree, this establishes that the estimate is unstable, not which one is right. Only the 49 disclosed-salary rows are ground truth — and the internship finding, which needs no external referee, because the estimator is being measured against its own written rule.

Cost. 150 re-estimates across six parallel workers. The full board would have been 4,256.
